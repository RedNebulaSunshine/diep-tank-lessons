How our tanks do what they do

People keep asking how the Halloween tanks pump, bite, watch, swing and fade. None of it is a mod: every trick is built from the three things the sandbox tank editor gives you, barrels, parts and auto turrets, set up in ways the stock tanks never use.

Each lesson below is one trick. It comes with a tiny demo tank: step through how it is put together in a mock of the editor (same panels, same field names), press Play to see it move, then download the pack or copy the tank and open it in the real editor to poke at the numbers yourself.

Start with lesson 00 if you have never looked at the Layers panel; otherwise jump to whatever you were wondering about.

Importing a lesson pack

  1. In the editor's pack screen press Import pack and choose the .diep-pack file (or drop it on the window).
  2. Pick Import as new pack. (Add to this pack also works and puts the demo tank into the pack you are editing.)
  3. Select the tank and press Play to spawn as it. Lesson tanks sit at level 1 under the stock Tank, so they also show up as an upgrade.

Or press Copy tank JSON on a lesson, open a pack of yours in the editor and press Ctrl+V. All fifteen demo tanks are also in one pack: tank-lessons.diep-pack.

The demos here are a sketch of the game, not the game: speeds and timings are approximate, and the editor's own preview does not animate at all. What the field names and numbers do is exact.

Lesson 00
How the editor draws a tank
Barrels, parts and auto turrets are stacked in layers, and the body splits what draws over it from what draws under it.
Lesson 01
A face that keeps the team colour
Paint anything you like over the body, as long as the body itself stays on Team color and shows through somewhere.
Lesson 02
Drawing with barrels
A barrel that fires nothing is a rectangle you can put anywhere, hair-thin or fanned out, even across the middle of the face.
Lesson 03
Spinning parts and a pulsing light
Spin speed turns a part on its own, and two stars spinning against each other read as a light that pulses.
Lesson 04
Parts that pump
A wing or a leg that moves is a barrel firing a harmless speck, because every barrel that fires slides back on the shot.
Lesson 05
Eyes that watch
An auto turret turns toward the nearest enemy. Make its disc the eyeball and let the pupil ride on it.
Lesson 06
A tail that swings
A turret with a narrow arc, facing backward, mostly rests; when the tank turns it lags and settles back, and anything riding it swings.
Lesson 07
A hand that follows the cursor
An auto turret with Range 0 that aims with the cursor rests at its Facing, swings to the cursor while you hold fire, and drops back when you let go.
Lesson 08
Jaws that bite
Two cursor pivots resting apart swing together when you hold fire, and invisible barrels along the jaws hurt whatever is caught between them.
Lesson 09
Limbs that live
Give a cursor pivot a Range as well, and the limb twitches toward passing enemies while you are idle and follows the cursor while you fire.
Lesson 10
A body that trails behind
An invisible rear barrel that always fires a shot with no speed leaves a segment wherever the tank was, so a chain follows every bend.
Lesson 11
A dash on right click
An invisible barrel pointing backward fires a harmless shot with huge Recoil, and the kick throws the tank forward.
Lesson 12
Hiding the body, keeping a hitbox
Shrink the body to a dot and give the figure a hitbox of its own with a Collidable part.
Lesson 13
Shots that are pictures
A projectile has its own layers, so a bullet can be a bat, an egg, a skull or a spiky star with an eye.
Lesson 14
Ghosts that fade
Invisibility is three numbers, and parts marked Visible while invisible stay drawn while the rest of the tank is gone.
